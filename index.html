Foundations of NLP: Paper Videos

The task

Each team explains one paper from the list below in a YouTube video.

  • Format: a Zoom recording or a screencast with slides.
  • Length: maximum 15 minutes.
  • Publishing: upload the video to your own YouTube channel.
  • Submission: send the link by email.

Optional extra: a short vertical video (no longer than 3 minutes) that presents the core idea of the paper in a simple form.

Core papers

  1. Shannon (1948). A Mathematical Theory of Communication (Part I only) Markov chains, entropy, n-gram approximations of English
  2. Bengio, Ducharme, Vincent, Jauvin (2003). A Neural Probabilistic Language Model Neural language modelling, learned embeddings
  3. Mikolov, Chen, Corrado, Dean (2013). Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space, together with Mikolov et al. (2013). Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality Distributional semantics, word2vec, negative sampling
  4. Pennington, Socher, Manning (2014). GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation Count-based vs. predictive embeddings
  5. Sennrich, Haddow, Birch (2016). Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units Tokenization, byte-pair encoding
  6. Sutskever, Vinyals, Le (2014). Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks LSTMs, encoder-decoder
  7. Bahdanau, Cho, Bengio (2015). Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate Attention
  8. Vaswani et al. (2017). Attention Is All You Need Transformer
  9. Devlin, Chang, Lee, Toutanova (2019). BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding Masked (autoencoding) objective, pretrain and fine-tune
  10. Radford, Narasimhan, Salimans, Sutskever (2018). Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training Autoregressive pretraining (GPT-1)

Optional papers

  1. Turing (1950). Computing Machinery and Intelligence, together with Searle (1980). Minds, Brains, and Programs What "understanding" means
  2. Collobert et al. (2011). Natural Language Processing (Almost) from Scratch Multitask learning
  3. Levy, Goldberg (2014). Neural Word Embedding as Implicit Matrix Factorization Link between word2vec and pointwise mutual information
  4. Peters et al. (2018). Deep Contextualized Word Representations Contextual embeddings (ELMo)
  5. Brown et al. (2020). Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (main sections only) In-context learning, scale (GPT-3)
